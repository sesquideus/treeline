Another well-defined measure of a mountain's dominance in its vicinity is the highest visible with respect to the local horizon. For peak A, we take every other peak in the database and compute the vertical angle β, filter out those for which it is positive, and mark the one with the highest value as the parent of the peak.

Compared to isolation, this forms many isolated trees – isolated dominant peaks often have a completely clean horizon and all other visible peaks, if any, are below the tangential plane. The horizon measure therefore does not result in a complete hierarchy.

Also unlike plain slope, this hierarchy is not that shallow and results in fewer surprises. However, there are again at least two posible definitions: one is purely geometric, which is well-defined and does not need any other parameters; another also takes atmospheric refraction into account. Currently we only store values for the standard value of c = 0.14.

By default, children are sorted in descending order by angle: peaks for which the angle is high are likely to be minor sub-summits, while peaks that are just enough far away to reach a small but positive values are likely relatively important, independent summits.
