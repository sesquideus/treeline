Another well-defined measure of a mountain's dominance in its vicinity is the highest visible with respect to the local horizon. For every other peak in the database we compute the vertical angle β, filter out those for which it is positive, and mark the one with the highest value as the local parent. and find the one for which it is largest

Compared to isolation, this forms many isolated trees – isolated dominant peaks often have a completely clean horizon and all other peaks, if any, are below the tangential plane. Horizon measure therefore does not result in a complete hierarchy (unless negative angles are also allowed, but this does not make much sense for very remote islands).

Also unlike plain slope, this hierarchy is not that shallow and results in fewer surprises.

By default, children are sorted in descending order by angle: peaks for which the angle is high are likely to be minor sub-summits, while peaks that are just enough far away to reach a small but positive values are likely relatively important, independent summits.
